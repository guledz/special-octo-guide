From programme strategy to field implementation — the operational structure required to build, manage and sustain Harar's digital visibility ecosystem.

Extends the main initiative proposal Pilot-oriented Planning framework — not a procurement order

Section 01

Operating Model

The programme runs as a layered operating model. Each layer has a defined function, a defined owner and a defined output that feeds the layer below it. Information flows downward as instructions and upward as verified data and reports.

  1. 01
    Mandate

    City Administration / Programme Governance

    Provides the mandate, approves scope, budget and data-governance rules, and receives programme reporting. Owns the decision to proceed from pilot to scale.

  2. 02
    Direction

    Programme Management

    Converts the approved mandate into plans, schedules, budgets, risk controls and team coordination. Owns delivery performance.

  3. 03
    Collection

    Field Operations

    Identifies businesses, verifies information, obtains consent, captures location data and conducts baseline digital audits on site.

  4. 04
    Production

    Digital Production

    Turns field material into finished assets: edited photography, business descriptions, brand assets, listings and web content.

  5. 05
    Adoption

    Business Support

    Trains and coaches owners and staff so the improved digital presence is maintained by the business after the intervention.

  6. 06
    Publication

    Digital Platform / Directory

    Hosts and maintains the city discovery layer — directory entries, map listings and web profiles — and keeps published information current.

  7. 07
    Learning

    Monitoring & Reporting

    Measures outputs and outcomes against the KPI framework, re-audits participating businesses and reports back to governance.

i

This is a functional model, not a fixed org chart. During the pilot, one person may hold more than one function, and some functions may be delivered by a partner organisation. Role separation should increase as the programme scales.

Section 02

Team Structure

Eleven functions are required to operate the programme. Responsibilities below describe what each function owns — not how many people must be hired. In a pilot, these functions are typically combined across a much smaller team.

01

Programme Lead

Accountability

  • Holds overall accountability for delivery and quality.
  • Liaises with the City Administration and programme governance.
  • Approves scope, budget, governance rules and publication policy.
  • Represents the programme to stakeholders and partners.

Reports to: Programme Governance / City Administration

02

Project / Operations Manager

Delivery

  • Runs day-to-day delivery across all workstreams.
  • Owns schedules, logistics, procurement and budget tracking.
  • Maintains the risk log and issue register.
  • Coordinates the field, production and data teams.

Reports to: Programme Lead

03

Field Coordinator

Field operations

  • Leads and supervises the field team.
  • Plans routes, clusters and business visit schedules.
  • Oversees the consent process and field conduct standards.
  • Checks field data quality before handover to production.

Reports to: Project / Operations Manager

04

Digital Auditors

Baseline & re-audit

  • Conduct baseline digital presence audits for each business.
  • Record findings using the standard audit scorecard.
  • Verify business information, location and contact details.
  • Re-audit after intervention using the same scorecard.

Reports to: Field Coordinator

05

Photographers / Content Creators

Visual production

  • Photograph businesses, premises, products and services.
  • Capture short video where the programme scope includes it.
  • Select and hand over images using the agreed file naming rules.
  • Work within consent and privacy conditions at all times.

Reports to: Field Coordinator

06

Web / GIS / Mapping Team

Platform & location

  • Creates and updates map listings, directory entries and web profiles.
  • Verifies coordinates and location accuracy.
  • Maintains the website and any directory infrastructure.
  • Checks links, mobile display and duplicate listings.

Reports to: Project / Operations Manager

07

Graphic / Brand Designer

Brand assets

  • Cleans up, redraws or standardises business logo and brand assets.
  • Produces listing visuals and social media templates.
  • Maintains visual consistency across published content.

Reports to: Digital Production

08

Data / Documentation Officer

Records & database

  • Enters and maintains records in the business master database.
  • Files consent records and field documentation.
  • Runs duplicate checks and data hygiene passes.
  • Manages backups and the archive structure.

Reports to: Project / Operations Manager

09

Training & Community Engagement Officer

Adoption

  • Delivers training and coaching to business owners and staff.
  • Prepares training materials and simple how-to guides.
  • Handles community liaison and multilingual communication needs.
  • Collects feedback from participating businesses.

Reports to: Programme Lead

10

Monitoring & Evaluation Officer

Measurement

  • Maintains the KPI framework and data collection tools.
  • Analyses progress and produces periodic reports.
  • Runs post-intervention review points.
  • Documents lessons learned for scale-up.

Reports to: Programme Lead

11

IT / Technical Support

Infrastructure

  • Sets up and maintains devices, accounts and connectivity.
  • Manages access permissions and account security.
  • Runs backup routines and restores when required.
  • Resolves technical issues for the field and production teams.

Reports to: Project / Operations Manager

Section 06

Field Workflow

The standard sequence followed for every business in the programme. Steps 1–6 are field activities, steps 7–11 are production and monitoring activities, and step 12 closes the loop.

  1. Select Business
  2. Verify Business Information
  3. Collect Location Data
  4. Conduct Digital Presence Audit
  5. Photograph Business
  6. Collect Business Description / Services
  7. Prepare Digital Assets
  8. Improve / Create Digital Presence
  9. Publish / Update Approved Information
  10. Quality Check
  11. Monitor Performance
  12. Report Results
i

Consent precedes capture. No photography, data entry or publication may proceed for a business until its consent record is documented and linked to the business ID.

Section 07

Business Onboarding System

Eight stages take a business from first identification to ongoing monitoring. Each stage has a responsible team, required information, a defined output and a quality-control checkpoint. Expand a stage to view its detail.

  1. Identify
  2. Verify
  3. Consent
  4. Audit
  5. Document
  6. Produce
  7. Publish
  8. Monitor
01 Identify

Responsible team

Field Operations — Field Coordinator with Digital Auditors.

Required information

  • Business name as commonly known
  • Category or trade
  • Approximate location or cluster
  • Owner or manager contact where available

Output

A candidate business record with a provisional business ID, added to the intake list.

Quality-control checkpoint

Duplicate check against the master database before the business is scheduled for a visit.

02 Verify

Responsible team

Field Operations — Digital Auditors, supervised by the Field Coordinator.

Required information

  • Legal or trading name confirmation
  • Owner or manager confirmation of details
  • Category, services and products
  • Opening information
  • Location confirmation on site

Output

A verified business record with verification status set and evidence noted.

Quality-control checkpoint

Verification status field completed, recording who verified, when, and by what method.

03 Consent

Responsible team

Field Coordinator with the Training & Community Engagement Officer.

Required information

  • Documented consent for data collection
  • Documented consent for photography
  • Documented consent for publication
  • Preferred contact channel
  • Any publishing restrictions requested by the business

Output

A consent record linked to the business ID and filed in both physical and digital form.

Quality-control checkpoint

Consent documented and confirmed before any photography or publication takes place.

04 Audit

Responsible team

Field Operations — Digital Auditors.

Required information

  • Existing search and map listings
  • Existing website or web profile
  • Social media presence
  • Accuracy of contact and location information
  • Quality and availability of imagery

Output

A baseline digital audit scorecard recorded using qualitative levels.

Quality-control checkpoint

Audit reviewed by the Field Coordinator for completeness and consistency of level assignment.

05 Document

Responsible team

Photographers with the Data / Documentation Officer.

Required information

  • Photographs of premises, products and services
  • Business description in the owner's own words
  • Services and product list
  • Brand assets where they exist

Output

Raw assets filed under the business ID using the standard folder and naming structure.

Quality-control checkpoint

Image quality, file naming and metadata checked on the same day as capture.

06 Produce

Responsible team

Digital Production — editing, design and copy.

Required information

  • Selected and edited images
  • Final business description
  • Cleaned logo or brand assets
  • Category and service tags

Output

An approved publishing package ready for listings, directory and website use.

Quality-control checkpoint

Internal review against the content checklist before anything is published.

07 Publish

Responsible team

Web / GIS / Mapping Team.

Required information

  • Approved publishing package
  • Verified business data
  • Verified map coordinates
  • Consent status confirmation

Output

Live map listing, directory entry or web profile for the business.

Quality-control checkpoint

Link testing, mobile display check and duplicate listing check immediately after publication.

08 Monitor

Responsible team

Monitoring & Evaluation Officer with Digital Auditors.

Required information

  • Published listing and profile URLs
  • Baseline audit record
  • Update and change log

Output

A post-intervention audit and a short performance note for the business record.

Quality-control checkpoint

Re-audit conducted at the agreed interval using the same scorecard as the baseline.

Section 08

Digital Audit System

The audit uses qualitative levels rather than invented numeric scores. The same scorecard is used before and after intervention so that change can be compared honestly.

Not Present Basic Developing Good Strong
Google / Search visibility

Whether the business can be found through search for its name, trade or location.

Maps presence

Whether the business appears on maps, and whether its pin location is correct.

Business information accuracy

Correctness of name, category, services and opening information across listings.

Website

Whether a website or web profile exists, loads correctly and works on mobile.

Social media

Presence, activity and consistency of social profiles linked to the business.

Photography

Availability, quality and usefulness of images representing the business.

Branding

Availability and consistent use of a recognisable logo or visual identity.

Contact information

Whether phone, messaging or email contact details are present and correct.

Location information

Clarity and accuracy of the physical address, landmark references and coordinates.

Customer discovery

How easily a customer can find, understand and contact the business digitally.

Digital accessibility

Whether published information is readable on mobile, legible, and available in the languages customers use.

Stage 1

Before Audit

Baseline digital presence recorded for each business using the qualitative scorecard. Establishes the starting point.

Stage 2

Intervention

Targeted improvements delivered: photography, verified information, listings, brand assets, training and publication.

Stage 3

After Audit

The same scorecard is applied again at the agreed interval to record change and identify what still needs work.

!

No official scoring values are defined here. The levels shown are qualitative and intended for consistent field judgement. If the main proposal defines a numeric scoring model, that model takes precedence and this template should be aligned to it.

Privacy and access control. The master database contains business contact information and consent records. Section 14

Infrastructure Requirements

Six physical and technical requirements must be in place before field operations begin.

01 Office / Coordination Space

A base for the programme team: workstations, secure storage for equipment and physical records, a meeting area for team coordination and a private space for training or business conversations. Shared or partner-hosted space is acceptable during the pilot if it meets the storage and access requirements.

02 Digital Production Space

A quiet workspace for image editing, content preparation and publishing. Requires reliable power, a stable internet connection, adequate lighting and controlled access so that unpublished business material is not left visible or unattended.

03 Field Operations

Practical requirements for field work: transport to and between business clusters, a means of carrying and protecting equipment, weather protection, and a clear daily route plan agreed with the Field Coordinator. Field teams must carry programme identification at all times.

04 Connectivity

Reliable internet is required at the coordination base for uploads, database work and reporting. Field connectivity should be assumed to be intermittent: forms must work offline and synchronise when a connection is available. Do not design field processes that require a continuous connection.

05 Power

Power backup at the coordination and production base to prevent data loss and interrupted uploads. In the field, power banks are required for every team member, and charging routines must be part of the daily plan rather than an afterthought.

06 Storage

Two forms of storage are required: physical storage for consent forms and field documentation, and digital storage for assets and the master database. Digital storage must follow a primary-plus-backup rule — one working copy and one backup copy, with backups verified on a regular schedule.

Section 15

Security & Data Governance

The programme handles business contact information, consent records and unpublished material. These controls are the minimum standard for handling it responsibly.

Access & accounts

  • User access permissions assigned by role, not by convenience.
  • Strong passwords required on all programme accounts.
  • Shared accounts avoided; individual accounts traceable to a person.
  • Access reviewed and revoked promptly when a role ends.

Data handling

  • Controlled access to the master database.
  • Consent records maintained and linked to each business ID.
  • Data verification before publication.
  • Secure file storage for unpublished material.

Continuity

  • Regular backups on a defined schedule.
  • Backups stored separately from working files.
  • Backup integrity checked, not assumed.
  • Version control on key documents and datasets.

Devices & retention

  • Device protection: screen locks, encryption where available.
  • Devices not left unattended with unpublished material accessible.
  • Archive policy defining what is retained, for how long, and what is deleted.
  • Withdrawal of consent must be actionable — published content removed or amended.

Section 17

Operational Phasing

Eight operational phases, from mobilisation to scale-up. The phase structure below should be aligned with the official Implementation Roadmap in the main proposal.

  1. 01

    Setup & Mobilisation

    Confirm scope and governance, establish the coordination base, agree data and consent rules, and prepare procurement.

  2. 02

    Team Training

    Train the field team on verification, consent, audit scoring, photography standards and data entry. Run a small supervised test visit.

  3. 03

    Business Mapping

    Identify and verify businesses in the target cluster. Build the initial master database and confirm location data.

  4. 04

    Digital Audits

    Complete baseline digital audits for all participating businesses using the standard scorecard.

  5. 05

    Content Production

    Photograph businesses, prepare descriptions, clean brand assets and build the publishing packages.

  6. 06

    Digital Implementation

    Publish listings, map entries, directory profiles and website content. Deliver owner and staff training.

  7. 07

    Monitoring & Optimisation

    Re-audit participating businesses, track KPIs, fix data and content gaps and correct issues found in the field.

  8. 08

    Evaluation & Scale-up

    Evaluate outputs and outcomes, document lessons, finalise the scale toolkit and present the case for expansion.

!

Align with the official roadmap. Phase names, durations and sequencing must match the approved Implementation Roadmap in the main proposal. If they differ, the proposal's structure takes precedence and this page should be updated.

Section 18

Scaling Model

The programme is designed to start small, prove the operating model, and expand only when the evidence supports it.

Pilot

One district or cluster, approximately 50–100 businesses. The purpose is to test the operating model, not to maximise coverage.

↓

Evaluate

Measure outputs and outcomes against the KPI framework. Identify what worked, what did not, and what the evidence actually supports.

↓

Improve

Fix operational gaps in workflow, team structure, tooling, data quality and training before expanding.

↓

Expand

Extend to additional districts or clusters using the refined model, scaling team size and production capacity accordingly.

↓

Citywide System

A sustainable digital visibility infrastructure for Harar, maintained beyond the programme period by the responsible institutions.

!

The pilot scale is a planning framework, not a guaranteed target. The number of businesses in the pilot must be confirmed against the approved scope, available budget and field capacity. Progression to each subsequent stage is a governance decision based on evidence.

Section 20

Operational Principles

Six principles govern how the programme operates in the field and how decisions are made when the handbook does not cover a specific situation.

Accuracy

Use verified information. If a detail cannot be confirmed with the business, it does not get published.

Consistency

Use standardised processes, forms and scorecards so that results are comparable across businesses and teams.

Accessibility

Design for mobile and multilingual users. Published information must be readable on the devices customers actually use.

Privacy

Respect consent and data protection. Consent is a condition of participation, not a formality.

Sustainability

Build systems that can continue beyond the pilot, with skills transferred to businesses and institutions.

Scalability

Design processes that can expand citywide without being rebuilt from scratch at each new district.
